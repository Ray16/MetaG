# Operational resource-recovery amendment

The first operational run was interrupted after other users' live processes
appeared on both gate-reserved GPUs. Six complete molecular records, one CUDA
out-of-memory failure, two interrupted inputs without complete result files and
all unstarted slots are retained. The OOM occurred with another process occupying
most device memory. These are not experimental prediction errors. The initial
run supplies no completed population-availability or isolated-runtime result.

Both original worker/predictor trees were verified absent, including process
start identities and inherited run markers. No other user's process was stopped.
The six complete records remain valid recorded molecular outputs; they are not
silently relabelled as an uncontended runtime measurement or merged into a fresh
restored molecular bank.

The separately frozen recovery repeats **all 115 raw inputs**, rather than a
subset chosen by an energy, prediction error or successful outcome. The 32 source
IDs, input structures, capacities, numerical/physical settings and acceptance
criteria are unchanged byte for byte. The worker now checks live GPU ownership
throughout each child and stops only its own tree on observed external contention.
Transient observation failures are logged and retried; they are not terminal
inference failures. The original deadline and eight-GPU-hour ceiling are shared
across both phases, with no reset and at most two MetaG workers concurrently.

This is a documented resource amendment, not the originally uninterrupted run.
Any availability interval is conditional on the restored operational profile and
requires complete restored opportunities without resource interruption or common
budget censoring. Original and restored outcomes and timings remain separate;
aggregate resource accounting includes both. Neither phase supplies experimental
accuracy, complete aqueous-ensemble validation, a production-policy choice or a
claim of a genome-scale walltime speedup. The pre-computation recovery manifest
contains no recovery quantum outputs.
